How it reads the signal

Type into the Received signal field; it is focused when the page opens and works like any text field, so you can move the cursor, select, paste and delete. Everything you type is kept exactly as typed. Spaces and slashes are treated as separators and skipped. The decoder needs exactly two other symbols: if they are . and - it reads them as written; otherwise the first one becomes symbol A and the second symbol B, and it tries both ways round, keeping whichever matches real bunker grids. If three or more different symbols are in the signal, it shows which ones and waits until you remove or replace the extras. Backspace always removes the last symbol; hold it to remove several. On a phone, use the Dot, Dash and Back buttons.

Each digit is five symbols. The broadcast sends any number of bunkers, one after another, and then starts over. Each is sent as its grid, three digits for the column and three for the row (counted from the top), sometimes followed by a four-digit PIN; the decoder recognises which format it is hearing. You can start listening at any point: it works out where digits and bunkers begin, lines the digits up with the known bunker grids, and adds each new bunker as it comes. When a bunker comes round again with the same grid and PIN, the loop is complete: the list stops growing, and later passes fill gaps and confirm digits.

Mistakes: the 23 bunker grids are known exactly, so the decoder lines the signal up against them. A missed, doubled or misheard symbol is corrected where it happened instead of shifting everything after it, and a digit it had to correct gets a wavy underline in the decoded view. A line break (or the long pause in a recording) always starts the next bunker, so a mistake cannot spill into the next one. The decoded view under the typing field shows how it is reading your signal: one tint per digit, a mark where the grid row, the PIN and each bunker start, and the bunker's colour. Tidy into rows rewrites the signal with one bunker per line, keeping your symbols.

What this page keeps, and where: your typed signal and settings are stored in this browser's local storage, and the recording (recorded or loaded, in full) in this browser's IndexedDB. Both stay on this device only, with no expiry of their own (the blue notice at the top lists when the browser itself may clear them), and a saved recording is reopened and analysed automatically next time. If you untick “Keep my data after I close the page”, the typed signal is kept only for this tab and the recording only in memory, and saved copies are removed. Clear signal removes the typed text; Delete recording removes the audio. Deleting this page or file does not clear the browser's storage, and exported files stay wherever you saved them. Nothing you type or record is sent anywhere: the page makes no network requests for it, and the fonts, map and MP3 encoder are built in.

While recording, the microphone hears everything around it, including speech in the room, and all of it is saved. The red Record button and the timer show when the microphone is on; Stop, or closing the page, turns it off.

Recording: choose what to record from. A microphone held near the speaker works anywhere. To record the computer's own sound without room noise, pick a loopback input (such as Stereo Mix, VB-Cable or BlackHole) or Computer sound, which shares a screen or tab together with its sound. Browsers cannot record straight from a speaker or headphone output. The page turns off echo cancellation and noise suppression so the beeps are not filtered away. It listens for the game's 988 Hz beep, learns how long dots, dashes and pauses are, and reads the beeps on the broadcast's rhythm: stray blips that fall off the beat are ignored (grey ticks), and a beep too faint to hear is filled in from the timing (dashed outline). A line break in the heard text marks the long pause between bunkers. The graph shades, in each bunker's colour, the stretches the decoder is using (lighter for the grid row and the PIN), so the bunker information stands out from noise and silence. If a beep was heard wrong, tap it on the graph and set it to dot or dash, or ignore it; a grey stray blip can be taken in the same way. Corrections are saved with the recording and survive a re-analysis; Undo all corrections removes them.
