How it reads the signal

Type into the Received signal field; it is focused when the page opens and works like any text field, so you can move the cursor, select, paste and delete. Everything you type is kept exactly as typed. Spaces and slashes are treated as separators and skipped. The decoder needs exactly two other symbols: if they are . and - it reads them as written; otherwise the first one becomes symbol A and the second symbol B, and it tries both ways round, keeping whichever matches real bunker grids. If three or more different symbols are in the signal, it shows which ones and waits until you remove or replace the extras. Backspace always removes the last symbol; hold it to remove several. On a phone, use the Dot, Dash and Back buttons.

Each digit is five symbols. The broadcast repeats three bunkers, each as ten digits: three for the grid column, three for the grid row (counted from the top), then a four-digit PIN. You can start listening at any point; the decoder works out where digits and records begin, and fills gaps when the broadcast loops around.

Your typed signal and your recording are kept in this browser on this device, so a refresh keeps them. The recording stays until you press Delete recording.

Recording: hold the microphone near the speaker. The page turns off echo cancellation and noise suppression so the beeps are not filtered away. It finds the beep's pitch on its own, learns how long dots, dashes and pauses are, and decodes while you record. A line break in the heard text marks the long pause between bunkers.
